# Story and script

A Short has one promise, one turn and one last image. If the viewer cannot say what the promise was after the
first sentence, the script is not ready.

## Pick the niche

| Niche | Preset | Promise |
|---|---|---|
| What-if / paradox explainer | `snap` or `twist` | one impossible-sounding consequence, then the mechanism that makes it true or false |
| Survival scenario choice | `choice` or `choice-long` | a lethal problem, four plausible responses, one decision the viewer must judge |

Both are **original stories**. Do not reuse a competitor's premise, title, beat order, character or joke. A
survival scenario can be inspired by a real engineering problem, but the crew, names and dialogue are ours.

## The idea screen

A usable idea passes all six:

1. **One sentence.** "What if X?" or "You have Y time to survive Z." If it needs a paragraph, cut it.
2. **Visual.** It can be staged with a room, a machine, a landscape, a face and a readable prop. No idea that
   only works as narration.
3. **Concrete stake.** A number, a deadline, a person, a place. "Something bad" is not a stake.
4. **Verifiable.** The factual spine has a source, or the whole thing is clearly labelled as a hypothetical.
5. **Advertiser-friendly.** No medical advice, gore, graphic violence, brands, real-person claims or politics.
6. **Fits the clock.** 24-35 s (`snap`), 46-57 s (`twist`), 46-59 s (`choice`), 75-150 s (`choice-long`).
   If the first draft is 70 s, it is either a `choice` to cut or a `choice-long` to build; do not split the
   difference.

## The hook

The first sentence is the title's promise, stated as fact, with the number or the impossible detail already in it.
No "hey guys", no logo, no weather report, no "in this video".

Good shapes:

- "Your Mars habitat just lost oxygen. You have two hours."
- "This sheet of paper is about to reach the Moon."
- "One power cell. Four systems. Pick one."

The first word must land at **0.00-0.45 s**. The first picture must already be moving or already hold the
problem. If the voice starts with a setup line, delete the line, not the shot.

## `twist` — the 46-57 s explainer

Six beats, about 115-148 words:

1. **Hook (0-3 s).** The promise, with the number.
2. **Setup (3-8 s).** Name the condition and the person. One concrete detail.
3. **Escalation (8-20 s).** Two or three consequences, each more visual than the last.
4. **Evidence (20-32 s).** The number or mechanism as a picture: a gauge, a stack, a counter, a diagram.
5. **Catch (32-45 s).** The fact that flips the setup. This is the reason the video exists.
6. **Payoff + loop (45-57 s).** Reframe the hook in one short sentence and cut back to the opening image.

## `snap` — the 24-35 s explainer

Four beats, about 62-90 words: hook, one setup, the turn, the loop. No evidence chapter; one number is enough.
`snap` is for a single clean paradox, not a compressed `twist`.

## `choice` — the 46-59 s survival Short

The viewer must feel the clock, understand four options, and want to argue in the comments. The card is the
format, not an end slate.

1. **Hook + stakes (0-8 s).** The failure, the deadline, the number of people. Establish the room.
2. **The failed fix (8-16 s).** Show why the obvious solution does not work. This makes the choice real.
3. **The four options (16-44 s).** One shot per option, each with a distinct place and a physical action:
   - **A** the fast, dangerous dash;
   - **B** the slow, fragile repair;
   - **C** the personal-risk walk;
   - **D** the long-shot signal.
   The voice names the option and the trade-off. The A-D card is drawn over the live shot.
4. **The catch (44-52 s).** One constraint that makes the choice hard: one power cell, one suit, one window.
5. **Vote + loop (52-59 s).** Ask for the pick in one line. Cut back to the opening gauge/room so the Short loops.

Mars `choice` example (the archived 58 s cut): 146 words, 57.6 s, 24 cuts/min, cards A-D at 37.9-53.7 s, loop
back to the O2 gauge for the last 3.9 s.
The four cards are **carried through the edit** as `seg.card`; the episode overlay draws them. Do not render the
cards into the set.

## `choice-long` — the 75-150 s story-led vote

This is the longer survival film: Wren is the POV protagonist, not a neutral narrator. She states the problem,
tries the obvious fix, walks the four options, makes the call on camera, and the film shows the cost. The vote
then judges her decision. 190-260 words, 20-36 shots, 75-150 s.

Seven beats:

1. **Hook + stakes (0-8 s).** Wren's promise, the number, the room. The failure is already happening.
2. **The failed fix (8-20 s).** The obvious repair dies on camera. This establishes the constraint and earns
   the four options.
3. **The four options (20-60 s).** One distinct place and one physical action each. The voice names the letter,
   the option and its cost. A single on-screen scoreboard (a power board, a gauge, a countdown) can carry the
   comparison so the viewer does not have to remember four separate facts.
4. **The catch (60-68 s).** One cell, five loads, one window. Put the constraint before the decision, not after.
5. **Wren's decision (68-78 s).** She chooses on camera, in her own voice. The line is short and declarative
   ("I choose C"), not a survey of the options.
6. **The judgement (78-84 s).** Show what the choice costs. Do not tell the viewer it was right; let the next
   card ask.
7. **Vote + loop (84-90 s).** Full-screen 4-up vote card: the spoken question, A-D labels, Wren's pick
   highlighted, then cut back to the opening frame.

Mars example (the 88.7 s picture, 89.2 s master): 225 words, 31 shots, 2,662 frames, 21 cuts/min, one-cell/five-loads
power board as the scoreboard, Wren chooses C (the surface walk), 7.6 s vote card at 81.1-88.7 s. The final card is
`WAS I RIGHT? A ROVER / B GREENHOUSE / C SURFACE / D SIGNAL`; C is highlighted because that is the choice the
film just made. The pinned comment repeats the same four options.

### Choice-card rules

- Four cards, one each, A-D. Each card gets a title of 2-3 words and a subtitle of 4-7 words.
- The card sits in the upper-left safe area, below the player's top bar. It must not cover the face or the
  physical action that explains the option.
- The card appears within 0-7 frames of the cut, eases in, and stays for the whole option.
- The voice says the letter and the option name. The caption can show the letter; the card is the bigger read.
- The final line asks for a vote without "smash subscribe" or engagement bait.

### Vote-card rules

- A `choice-long` ends on a full-screen 4-up card, not another A-D overlay on a live shot.
- It repeats the question the voice just asked and the four labels; highlight the letter Wren chose.
- It covers the caption band for its hold; the card carries the text, so hide the captions while it is up.
- Keep the face above the card. On 1080x1920 the card starts at 780 px; the eyes and mouth must sit above it.
- Hold 7-8 s, then cut back to the opening frame. The card is the last image, not a trailer.
- The pinned comment repeats the same four options and asks for a letter.

## Word budgets and pace

- `snap`: 62-90 words.
- `twist`: 115-148 words.
- `choice`: 115-155 words.
- `choice-long`: 190-260 words.
- Target 2.3-3.0 words per second after pause tightening. 146 words is about 57 s; 225 words is about 88 s.
- 20-30 cuts/min overall. Median shot 1.5-3.5 s. No shot over 5.5 s except the final vote-card hold (7-8 s).
  First 10 s at about 30 cuts/min.
- A 5.5 s hold needs internal action: a push-in, a light change, a hand prop, a screen update.

## The loop

The last image should be the first image again, or a deliberate echo of it. The cleanest loop is the same shot,
same camera, same set, played in reverse or re-entered at the top. The last line should land within about 0.5 s
of the end; no trailing silence.

## Facts

Every factual claim goes in `science`:

```json
{ "claim": "…", "working": "…", "source": "…", "status": "verified" }
```

- `verified`: the source states it.
- `arithmetic`: the number is calculated and the inputs are sourced.
- `hypothetical`: the claim is a premise, not a fact. Say so in the narration.
- If a source cannot be opened, cut the claim. A beautiful shot is not worth a false fact.

## Titles and thumbnails

The title is a promise, not a summary. Put the number or the impossible detail first. Keep it 5-11 words and
put one or two emojis at the end only. The first frame should be readable as a thumbnail without captions:
one face, one object, one colour conflict. No competitor titles, no "you won't believe", no fake urgency.

## Common story failures

- **Setup before hook.** The first 3 s explain the world instead of threatening the viewer.
- **Four options that are not different.** If A and B are both "run", the vote is fake.
- **The catch arrives after the vote.** Put the constraint before the final ask.
- **The POV drifts.** In `choice-long`, Wren must make the call. If Voss or Chen decides, the vote has no owner.
- **The vote card is an afterthought.** If it covers her face, hides the caption band with no replacement text,
  or repeats a different set of labels than the options, rebuild it.
- **The ending explains the joke.** Cut the last sentence and let the loop do the work.
- **Too many words.** If the voice is over the preset budget (155 for `choice`, 260 for `choice-long`), the idea
  is too big for the format.
- **No physical action.** A shot of a person talking is not a shot; give them a prop, a screen, a door, a lever.
